Olympiads

School olympiads are a well-established tradition in Russia, with competitions held in nearly every subject and drawing large numbers of talented high schoolers, in part because strong results come with preferential admission to university. I took part in these competitions not only for the admission benefits, but because I loved the problems themselves: math puzzles to crack and compete over with friends, and economics questions that opened a window onto real-world phenomena from every corner of life. Just as rewarding was the community itself — a group of sharp, curious peers who kept pushing each other to go further.

Having placed among the winners in several math and economics olympiads, which helped me get into my top-choice undergraduate program, I chose to stay involved in this close-knit community, contributing to it from the other side of the table — as a problem writer and grader. During my undergraduate studies, I helped write and grade problems for the NES High School Economics Competition and served on the jury of the final round of the All-Russian Schoolchildren's Olympiad, the most prestigious such competition in the country.

Today, I lead the organization of the NES High School Economics Competition (site in Russian). I oversee the design of the competition itself — working with a team of students to develop problem ideas and turn them into finished assignments — and then lead the grading process once submissions come in. The goal, ultimately, is to help new generations of high schoolers fall in love with economics the way I did: through solving genuinely interesting problems.
